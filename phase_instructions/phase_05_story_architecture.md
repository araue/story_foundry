# Phase 5: Story Architecture and Chapter Briefs

## Purpose and authority

Phase 5 decides what the story and each chapter must accomplish. Phase 8
decides how approved decisions become prose. Neither phase may override source
constraints, approved assumptions, or an explicit editor decision.

Planning files are author knowledge. Reader knowledge comes only from the
manuscript. If architecture requires a new premise, return to Phase 4.

## Novel-scale architecture

Create and maintain:

- `story/vision.md` — why this project exists beyond resolving plot;
- `story/themes.md` — tensions the story tests rather than declares solved;
- `story/questions.md` — mysteries answered, partly answered, or preserved;
- `story/ending.md` — ending, costs, and lasting conditions;
- `story/outline.md` — causal chapter sequence.

Build from purpose, themes, mystery policy, and ending to character and
institutional movement, then chapter sequence and individual briefs. Each
chapter must create an irreversible change that affects what follows.

## Outline entry

```markdown
## Chapter N — Title

- **Purpose:** Why is this chapter necessary?
- **Viewpoint:** Who experiences it, and why?
- **Visible conflict:** What incompatible aims drive it?
- **Irreversible change:** What differs at the end?
- **Reader burden:** What one major new subject must become clear?
- **Character and power movement:** Who changes, and what dependency changes?
- **Theme:** Which approved tension is tested?
- **Link forward:** What condition does the ending create?
```

## Reader-knowledge and introduction design

For each consequential person, practice, place, technology, relationship, or
term, classify it as established, needing reminder, new, deferred, or genuinely
unknown. Deferred or unknown larger meaning never excuses confusion about what
it does in the present scene.

Introduce material through a concrete encounter, immediate function and stakes,
name and necessary context, a second dimension, then consequential use. Give a
chapter only as many unfamiliar elements as it can explain naturally.

## Required chapter brief

Prepare one brief at a time in `story/chapter_briefs/`:

```markdown
# Chapter N Architecture Brief

## Chapter Job
- Purpose:
- Viewpoint:
- Visible conflict:
- Irreversible ending change:
- Link to the next chapter:

## Plain-Language Chapter Core
Who wants what, what do they do, why do they disagree, what does it cost, and
what changes? Avoid planning shorthand.

## Reader Starting Point
What relevant facts has prior manuscript prose established? What needs reminder?

## Reader Handoff
- Primary new subject:
- What the reader must understand and why it matters now:
- Names or terms requiring immediate context:
- What may be deferred or remains unknown:

## Introduction Sequence
For each new or deepened element: encounter, immediate function, needed context,
and the decision or consequence that makes it matter.

## Concrete Realization
What will the reader see or hear? What does it mean in ordinary language, whose
need or risk does it affect, and what decision makes it consequential?

## Scene Plan
For each scene: place, starting situation, viewpoint objective and pressure,
visible event, information supplied before use, change, and causal transition.

## Character and Thematic Movement
What changes in judgment, relationship, power, or dependency? What question does
the concrete conflict embody?

## Continuity and Approval Check
Which assumptions, arcs, cast dossiers, and world files constrain the chapter?
Does anything need a new decision?
```

## Handoff check

Before Phase 8, confirm that a reader can follow the action, conflict, stakes,
and change using only previous prose and deliberate introductions; every new
term has immediate context; major abstractions correspond to visible actions or
risks; transitions and aftermath are planned; preserved mysteries are genuinely
unknown; and all speculative premises are approved.
